# Revive Us Again

## Introduction

In 2008, we began meeting at a church to pray with a certain lady. Every morning at around 7 a.m., I would walk from home to the church, which was almost a stone's throw away. We would meet and pray for about 30 minutes every day. As we continued praying each morning, others heard how God had been moving in our meetings. We were experiencing the power of the Holy Spirit, and people were experiencing deliverance from demonic strongholds and generational patterns. Not long after, the encounters we were experiencing in the prayer meetings began to manifest in the main church services. In no time, meetings that began with two people started to attract many people.

On the weekends in particular, students would come and join us in prayer; they came in numbers. Many of them encountered the power of God, and they could not remain the same. When they went back to their school, they shared the gospel of Jesus with boldness and courage.

This school, Hillcrest, which I had attended myself, was a national school where the brightest students in Zambia were enrolled. When I was a student there, there was a lot of darkness at the school because of occult practices. But when these students started attending our Saturday prayers, they caught the fire so much that darkness could not stand them. Students would be in class, and suddenly demons would begin manifesting. For a period of time, classes had to be halted, and parents were called in to come and witness what was happening. The school then decided that its students would no longer be allowed to come and pray at our church. For a moment, this school had encountered a pocket of revival, and it all started with a few students hungry for the presence of God, tired of the ordinary, and wanting more.

Throughout history, various communities in various dispensations have encountered the power and presence of God. There have been pockets of revival in Europe, in Asia, in North America and in other regions of the world.

Take, for instance, Wales in 1904. During a meeting led by Seth Joshua, a young man by the name of Evan Roberts experienced the power of God, and he began to cry, "Bend me, O Lord, bend me, O Lord." His cry was a total spiritual surrender of his personal will, pride and stubbornness to the Holy Spirit. It was his way of saying, "It's not about me; it's all about You. Lord, I die to myself so that You can live through me." The posture of repentance and seeking the Lord's face that Evan assumed was the cornerstone of the Welsh Revival. It is estimated that almost 100,000 people gave their lives to Christ during the revival. It was said that crime fell so sharply that some courts had no rape or murder cases to hear. In the coal mines, it was reported that the pit ponies stopped obeying instructions. Before the revival, the miners had always used foul language to give commands, and that was all the ponies were used to. During the revival, the miners changed their language, and the ponies could not understand the instructions.

Just after the Welsh Revival, another revival began on Azusa Street in Los Angeles, led by William Joseph Seymour. The revival happened at a time of systematic discrimination against people of colour in the United States. Slavery had ended only about forty years earlier, Black people were treated as second-class citizens, and their voices had been suppressed. During this time, God raised up a man of God, William Seymour. It is a true depiction of 1 Corinthians 1:27: God has chosen the foolish things of the world to confound the wise. The prayer meetings began in a house. As they continued to pray, seek the face of God, sing and share the word of God, the meetings grew larger and larger until they moved to an old building on Azusa Street that had once been an African Methodist Episcopal church and had since been used as a stable and warehouse. There they experienced the power of God. It was reported that people recovered from blindness, deafness, paralysis, tuberculosis and severe tumours. Crutches, canes and braces were famously hung on the walls of the mission as physical evidence of those healed. And in a divided nation, people of different races worshipped together at Azusa Street. This small mission in Los Angeles was hungry for a move of God. They prayed passionately, fervently and sincerely, and God manifested His power.

There were many other revivals in history, but I wanted to share these examples to encourage you. In the past, people have called upon the Lord with genuine hearts; people have cried out to God, and God has heard them. And we can also experience an unprecedented move of God.

In my sermon today, I want to briefly define what a revival is. Then we will look at one biblical example of revival, the Day of Pentecost, and what we can learn from it: why revivals happen, and the place of the Holy Spirit in revival. Finally, I will close by identifying the posture God wants us to take as we prepare for a great outpouring of His Spirit.

## 1. What Is a Revival?

Let's begin with a definition. What is a revival?

The title of this sermon comes from Psalm 85:6: "Will you not revive us again, that your people may rejoice in you?" To revive something is to bring it back to life.

A revival is a sudden, sovereign outpouring of the Holy Spirit that awakens believers, exalts Jesus Christ, and sparks mass conversion and transformation in a community.

According to this definition, the Holy Spirit is at the centre of every revival. When there is an outpouring of the Spirit of God, it brings an awakening to believers, Jesus Christ is exalted, many souls are won over to the Kingdom of God, and through the supernatural manifestation of the Spirit of God, communities are transformed.

Revival is God's sovereign work, and we cannot manufacture it. But in Scripture and in history, God has poured out His Spirit on people who were praying and seeking His face.

## 2. One Biblical Example: Pentecost (Acts 1–2)

Now let's look at one biblical example of revival, the Day of Pentecost, and see how the Holy Spirit triggers each part of this definition.

### The Story

The story begins in Acts 1, in the days before Jesus was taken up to heaven.

> **Acts 1:4–8**
>
> 4 On one occasion, while he was eating with them, he gave them this command: "Do not leave Jerusalem, but wait for the gift my Father promised, which you have heard me speak about. 5 For John baptized with water, but in a few days you will be baptized with the Holy Spirit." 6 Then they gathered around him and asked him, "Lord, are you at this time going to restore the kingdom to Israel?" 7 He said to them: "It is not for you to know the times or dates the Father has set by his own authority. 8 But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit comes on you; and you will be my witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth."

When Jesus was about to depart, His last instruction to the disciples was that they should not leave Jerusalem and that they should wait for the gift of the Holy Spirit. After Jesus was taken up into heaven, the disciples obeyed Him and stayed in Jerusalem.

> **Acts 1:12–14**
>
> 12 Then the apostles returned to Jerusalem from the hill called the Mount of Olives, a Sabbath day's walk from the city. 13 When they arrived, they went upstairs to the room where they were staying. Those present were Peter, John, James and Andrew; Philip and Thomas, Bartholomew and Matthew; James son of Alphaeus and Simon the Zealot, and Judas son of James. 14 They all joined together constantly in prayer, along with the women and Mary the mother of Jesus, and with his brothers.

Then came the Day of Pentecost.

> **Acts 2:1–4**
>
> 1 When the day of Pentecost came, they were all together in one place. 2 Suddenly a sound like the blowing of a violent wind came from heaven and filled the whole house where they were sitting. 3 They saw what seemed to be tongues of fire that separated and came to rest on each of them. 4 All of them were filled with the Holy Spirit and began to speak in other tongues as the Spirit enabled them.

After spending time in corporate prayer, there was an outpouring of the Holy Spirit, and the Bible says He enabled them to speak in tongues. In the verses that follow, we are told that there was pandemonium in Jerusalem; people who had travelled from other nations could hear the disciples speaking in their own languages.

And so Peter stood up to preach the Gospel of Jesus. The Bible says in Acts 2:37–41:

> **Acts 2:37–41**
>
> 37 When the people heard this, they were cut to the heart and said to Peter and the other apostles, "Brothers, what shall we do?" 38 Peter replied, "Repent and be baptized, every one of you, in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of your sins. And you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. 39 The promise is for you and your children and for all who are far off—for all whom the Lord our God will call." 40 With many other words he warned them; and he pleaded with them, "Save yourselves from this corrupt generation." 41 Those who accepted his message were baptized, and about three thousand were added to their number that day.

When Peter finished preaching, the people's hearts were convicted of their sin, and they turned back to God. Three thousand were added to their number that day.

Look at what happened at Pentecost. Believers were awakened: the same Peter who had denied Jesus only weeks earlier now stood up and preached with boldness. Jesus Christ was exalted: Peter declared that "God has made this Jesus, whom you crucified, both Lord and Messiah" (Acts 2:36). There was a mass conversion: three thousand people were saved in one day. And a community was transformed. The new believers devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching, to fellowship and to prayer, and they shared their possessions with anyone in need. Acts 2:47 says that "the Lord added to their number daily those who were being saved." Every part of our definition happened at Pentecost.

### What We Learn: Why Do Revivals Happen?

What can we learn from Pentecost? First, it shows us why revivals happen. Revivals happen when hungry people pray together, constantly, for what God has promised.

Jesus had told the disciples to wait for "the gift my Father promised" (Acts 1:4). They could have chosen to wait passively. They could have gone back to their homes to eat and drink as they waited for the Holy Spirit to come. In fact, I think if they had done so, they would have delayed His coming. But the Bible says that when they returned to Jerusalem, they went upstairs to the room where they were staying, and "they all joined together constantly in prayer" (Acts 1:14). The word "constantly" tells us that they were praying continuously and consistently. If we want to experience a revival in our time, we need to take the same posture as the early church.

The Bible doesn't say they were praying individually in their homes. Personal prayer is important, but corporate prayer as the church is also essential. If you study any revival, you will notice that there was consistent and constant prayer before a great outpouring of the Spirit of God. At Azusa Street and in the Welsh Revival, the saints were praying. And they did not meet to pray selfish prayers. They were praying for the lost, and they were praying for God to transform their communities.

E. M. Bounds says, "What the Church needs today is not more machinery or better, not new organizations or more and novel methods, but men whom the Holy Ghost can use—men of prayer, men mighty in prayer."

Thus far I have lived in about six different countries, and I have noticed in all these places that prayer meetings are the least attended meetings in the church. If you call for a fund day or a barbecue, many people will attend, but when we call for prayer, very few people come. But as E. M. Bounds says, what the church needs to encounter an outpouring of the presence of God is men and women of prayer. As the Staffanstorp congregation, I want to challenge us to make our prayer meetings the most attended meetings. We can accomplish so much more for God's kingdom if we assume a posture of prayer as a church.

### What We Learn: The Place of the Holy Spirit in Revival

Prayer prepares the way, but it is the Holy Spirit who brings revival. So the second thing Pentecost shows us is the place of the Holy Spirit in revival.

Acts 2:4 says, "All of them were filled with the Holy Spirit." As our definition said, the Holy Spirit is at the centre of every revival. We will see that He is a Person, that He gives us power to be witnesses, and that He wants room in our lives and in our church.

**He is a Person**

Who is the Holy Spirit? The Holy Spirit is the third person of the Trinity. There are different misconceptions about the person of the Holy Spirit. One of the most common is the idea that the Spirit of God is an abstract, impersonal force, an "it." Those who view the Holy Spirit as an abstract or impersonal force will only ever experience Him in abstract ways.

The biblical evidence points us to the fact that the Holy Spirit is a person. Romans 8:27 says the Holy Spirit has a mind; Ephesians 4:30 says He has emotions; 1 Corinthians 12:11 says He has a will; 1 Corinthians 2:13 says He teaches; Acts 20:22–23 says He gives guidance; and John 14:16 says He is a comforter.

**He gives us power to be witnesses**

In Acts 1:8, Jesus points the disciples to an important truth: when the Holy Spirit comes on them, He is coming to empower them. He is not empowering them so that they can show off that they can be used by God. He is not empowering them so that they can control people. He is not empowering them for their personal gain. He says they will receive power so that they may be His witnesses.

Whenever there is an outpouring of the Holy Spirit, signs and wonders accompany the Spirit's move. It brings spiritual renewal to believers, but more importantly, it points the lost back to God. That is why three thousand people were saved on the Day of Pentecost.

**He wants relationship and room**

You cannot walk in the power of the Holy Spirit if you do not have a relationship with Him. This is why, if we want to see the manifestation of God's power in our lives and community, we need as individuals to have a relationship with Him. Remember, He is not an abstract force. He is a person who desires a deeper level of relationship and intimacy with us. For those who are married, imagine spending days without talking to your spouse, or waking up every morning without greeting and acknowledging your spouse. That relationship would break down. And many of us have lived our lives without daily communion with the Holy Spirit. He is always at the door of your heart, wanting to have fellowship with you.

This is why Jesus says to the Laodicean church in Revelation 3:20, "Here I am! I stand at the door and knock. If anyone hears my voice and opens the door, I will come in and eat with that person, and they with me."

Although this verse is often used to evangelise unbelievers, it was actually written to believers. The Laodicean church had become spiritually proud, self-sufficient and "lukewarm" (neither hot nor cold). Because of this attitude, Christ metaphorically stands outside the community's door, seeking re-entry and fellowship.

Sometimes we can be like the Laodicean church. We are too busy for the Holy Spirit. We are busy with the things of life, busy with work, busy with raising children. Yes, all these things are important, but they should not replace our fellowship with the Holy Spirit, for it is He who ushers in a revival. If we want to experience a personal revival, we have to prioritise the Holy Spirit in our lives.

As the church, if we want to experience a community and national revival, we have to give room to the Holy Spirit to use the church. For many churches in our generation, the Holy Spirit is standing at the door and knocking, because we have kept Him outside our meetings with our programs. We need to make room for Him to work in our midst. If the Holy Spirit wants us to spend a little more time in worship, we should let Him disrupt our schedule and church programs.

## 3. The Posture God Wants Us to Take

Finally, what posture does God want us to take as we prepare for a great outpouring of His Spirit?

I want you to notice that before the revival, they were meeting in an upper room, and there were only a few of them. Our prayer meetings in 2008 began with two people. Azusa Street began in a house. This should tell us that God can work with a few people to transform an entire city and an entire nation. Maybe some of you have been discouraged for a long time because we are just a small church in Staffanstorp. But I have come to encourage you that God just needs a few faithful people to work with. He can use our congregation to transform this city; He can use our congregation to transform this region; He can use our congregation to spark a mighty move of His presence.

We just need to be faithful in prayer, and we need to assume a posture of repentance. The Bible says in 2 Chronicles 7:14:

> If My people, who are called by My name, shall humble themselves and pray, and seek My face and turn from their wicked ways, then will I hear from heaven, and will forgive their sin and will heal their land.

This is the posture God wants us to take: we humble ourselves, we pray, we seek His face, and we turn from our wicked ways. And He has promised that if we do, He will hear from heaven, He will forgive our sins and He will heal our land. This is why Evan Roberts cried, "Bend me, O Lord," as a sign of surrender. We need to totally surrender our lives to Christ.

We need to cry out to the Lord this afternoon: Bend us, O Lord.

If we have been inconsistent in our prayer life, forgive us and bend us, O Lord.

If we have locked the Holy Spirit outside our church because we wanted to follow our own program and agenda, bend us, O Lord.

If we have not loved those who are different from us in our community, bend us, O Lord.

If we have not been faithful in evangelising the lost, forgive us and bend us, O Lord.

Our next prayer meeting is on [day and time]. Let's make it the most attended meeting in our church.

Let us pray, in the words of Psalm 85:6: "Will you not revive us again, that your people may rejoice in you?"
